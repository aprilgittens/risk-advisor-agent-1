# Boulder Innovations

## Company Overview

**Company Name:** Boulder Innovations  
**Industry:** Industrial Technology and Smart Infrastructure  
**Headquarters:** Boulder, Colorado, United States  
**Founded:** 2008  
**Employees:** 4,600  
**Annual Revenue:** $1.8 billion  
**Primary Markets:** United States, Canada, United Kingdom, and Western Europe  

Boulder Innovations develops smart building systems, industrial sensors, energy-management platforms, and connected infrastructure products for commercial and industrial customers.

The company combines hardware manufacturing, cloud software, embedded systems, and field services. Revenue is split across product sales, software subscriptions, maintenance contracts, and professional services.

---

## Business Operations

Boulder Innovations operates:

- 5 manufacturing facilities
- 3 hardware research and development centers
- 4 software engineering offices
- 7 regional service hubs
- 2 primary cloud environments

Its largest operations are located in:

- Boulder, Colorado
- Austin, Texas
- San Jose, California
- Raleigh, North Carolina
- Manchester, United Kingdom

Approximately 42% of annual revenue depends on connected hardware products and the cloud services required to operate them.

Many enterprise customers rely on Boulder Innovations products for building automation, energy monitoring, environmental controls, and equipment telemetry.

## Technology Environment

Boulder Innovations operates a hybrid technology environment that includes:

- Azure
- Microsoft 365
- GitHub
- Kubernetes
- IoT device-management platforms
- Customer-facing SaaS applications
- Embedded firmware
- Industrial gateways
- Remote monitoring systems

The company manages approximately 2.4 million connected devices deployed at customer locations.

Software updates and firmware patches are distributed remotely.

Known technology concerns include:

- Legacy firmware still operating on older customer devices
- Dependency on several open-source software packages
- Remote administrative access to customer-installed equipment
- Inconsistent patch adoption among customers
- Increasing API traffic from third-party integrations

## Cybersecurity Risk

Cybersecurity is considered one of Boulder Innovations' highest enterprise risks.

Current controls include:

- Multi-factor authentication
- Endpoint detection and response
- Centralized identity management
- Secure development lifecycle practices
- Annual penetration testing
- Security monitoring
- Incident response planning
- Device certificate authentication

Known concerns include:

- Large connected-device footprint
- Customer-controlled network environments
- Third-party integrations
- Legacy device firmware
- Software supply chain exposure

Two years ago, a vulnerability was discovered in an older generation of industrial gateways.

No confirmed exploitation was identified, but Boulder Innovations issued an emergency firmware update to approximately 185,000 devices.

## Supply Chain

Boulder Innovations depends on specialized electronic components, including:

- Microcontrollers
- Wireless modules
- Industrial sensors
- Secure elements
- Power-management chips

Approximately 28% of critical electronic components come from single-source suppliers.

Primary sourcing regions include:

- Taiwan
- South Korea
- Japan
- United States
- Germany

The company maintains between 30 and 45 days of inventory for most critical components.

A semiconductor shortage three years ago delayed shipments of several product lines for approximately six weeks.

## Property and Operational Exposure

Key operational concerns include:

- Electronics manufacturing equipment
- Prototype laboratories
